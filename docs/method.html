The method

Study the hand.
Trust the schedule.

Bihua is built on a small set of claims: producing a character is different from recognising it; retrieval is more useful than re-reading; and a memory model should decide when a character returns, not a streak counter.

Most character apps begin with recognition: show a character, show a meaning, ask the learner to tap or swipe. Bihua begins somewhere else. It asks the hand to produce the character from a cue, then asks the learner to judge what actually appeared on the page.

The short version

Write from memory → check the attempt → grade it yourself → let a memory model choose the next date. The guide fades, the answer stays hidden, and pronunciation is a separate task on a separate schedule.

1. The unit is the character

Bihua is not a vocabulary deck with a writing exercise attached. The character is the scheduled object. Words are context; readings and meanings are cues. That choice keeps the review queue honest: the learner is always asked to produce a glyph, not merely to recognise a word in a list.

Characters are also shared across scripts. 日, 人, and 上 are the same glyph in Simplified, Traditional, and Japanese. Bihua can share one writing schedule for an identical glyph while keeping the reading and meaning for each course separate. Sharing is opt-in because some learners want each script to feel like its own project.

Script purity

Simplified and Traditional are separate courses. Simplified-only forms do not appear in Traditional, traditional-only forms do not appear in Simplified, and shared glyphs stay in both. Traditional words use CEDICT word-level pairs rather than mechanical character substitution.

2. Production before recognition

Recognition is not the same skill as production. Seeing 愛 and knowing it means “love” does not mean the hand can produce the strokes in the right order. Bihua therefore makes writing the default review and asks for the character before showing the answer.

This is a retrieval-first design. The learner generates a response, then receives feedback. The app does not reveal the target first and ask for a confidence rating; that would turn a production task into a recognition task with extra steps.

Handwriting also engages motor planning, spatial sequencing, and visual monitoring. Those are not decorative extras. They are part of what makes character production a distinct skill, and they are why the canvas is the largest object on the screen.

3. The lesson loop: meet, trace, recall

A new character gets a short introduction, one guided trace, and then a recall attempt. Reviews skip the introduction and go straight to recall. The loop is deliberately short: the app is not trying to keep the learner in a lesson, it is trying to make the next review possible.

The trace step exists because a first attempt at an unfamiliar glyph is often a drawing exercise, not a retrieval exercise. One guided pass gives the hand a path to follow. After that, the guide fades and the learner has to generate the path.

4. The guide fades

A permanent trace is a crutch. Bihua starts with full strokes, moves to an outline, then to nothing, based on how many successful reviews the character has. The learner can always ask for the guide, but it is hidden by default during recall.

Settings can force a guide mode, and the learner can reveal the guide during recall. The default is a fade because the goal is independent production, not perfect tracing.

5. Spacing with FSRS-7

Each script has its own FSRS-7 schedule. FSRS is an open spaced-repetition model that estimates memory stability and difficulty and uses a target retrieval probability to choose the next interval. Bihua uses it as a practical scheduler, not as a promise about the mind.

In plain terms: a character that is easy to recall can wait longer; a character that is shaky comes back sooner. The learner’s grade is the input. The model handles the date. There is no streak to protect and no penalty for returning after a break.

Stability

How long the memory is expected to last. Successful recall increases it; a lapse reduces it.

Difficulty

How hard this item tends to be for this learner. It changes the effect of a grade.

Retrievability

The estimated chance of recall at a given moment. The scheduler uses it to choose the next date.

Vacation mode pauses new cards and reviews, then shifts due dates by the pause when it ends. The next seven days use shorter sessions so a return does not become an overdue avalanche. This is a compromise, not a second scheduler.

6. Two skills, two tasks

Writing and pronunciation are separate skills. Bihua keeps them separate instead of pretending one review can test both. Writing asks for the glyph. Pronunciation shows the character and asks for the reading. Either can appear in a review, depending on the learner’s settings and the card’s phase.

Separating the tasks avoids a common failure mode: a learner who can write 愛 but cannot say it, or can say it but cannot produce it, gets a single blended score that hides the gap. Here the gap is visible.

7. Errors are data, not failure

After a recall attempt, Bihua compares the drawing to the stroke guide where one exists. It can surface stroke count, order, reversals, missing or extra strokes, and a focused correction loop for the stroke that went wrong. The learner can replay the reference, undo, clear, or redraw.

The app does not auto-grade the character and call it objective. Recognition is evidence; the learner still chooses Again, Hard, Good, or Easy. That choice is what enters FSRS. The point is not to punish imperfection, it is to make the next attempt more informed.

A deliberate limit

Handwriting recognition is probabilistic and stroke guides are not available for every glyph. Bihua shows what it can verify and leaves the final judgement with the learner. It never pretends a recognition score is a complete assessment.

8. Context without clutter

Meaning is a cue, not a paragraph. Character detail holds the reading, meaning, level, strokes, frequency, variants, radical, components, etymology, related families, words, notes, and history. The review screen keeps only what the current task needs.

Two transfer practices sit outside the schedule. Word writing asks the learner to produce a word’s characters from its reading and meaning. Paper check asks for a character on real paper, then reveals the target for comparison. Both are optional; neither creates a card or XP.

Phonetic and semantic families are a reference layer, not a second curriculum. When a family is useful, it explains why characters look or sound related. When it is noisy, it stays out of the review.

9. Ownership, privacy, and limits

Bihua has no account and no analytics. Progress lives in a local SQLite database. A full zip backup includes settings and progress; CSV export covers review history. Optional network access is limited to handwriting/model downloads and local Wi-Fi sync. The app does not upload study data to a server.

The method has limits. It is not a clinical intervention, a replacement for a teacher, or a guarantee of literacy. Stroke data is incomplete for some rare glyphs. The scheduler is a model, not a measurement of the mind. The app is designed to be honest about those limits instead of hiding them behind a score.

What Bihua deliberately avoids

Streaks, leaderboards, coins, loss-framed reminders, auto-revealed answers, and a single blended “mastery” score. None of them are required to learn a character, and several of them make it harder to return after a break.

10. Using Bihua well

  • Write at a sustainable size. A wrist-sized canvas is usually better than a whole-arm one. Change the writing size in Settings if the square feels cramped.
  • Grade honestly. “Good” is not a reward. If the character only came back after seeing the guide, that is useful information for the scheduler.
  • Use the guide, then remove it. A hint is fine. Staying on the hint is not the goal.
  • Keep sessions short. Ten focused cards are better than a long session that ends in frustration.
  • Return after a break. Vacation mode and ease-back exist so a pause does not turn into a backlog.
  • Use words and paper as transfer. They are not scheduled, so use them when they help, not as a second job.
